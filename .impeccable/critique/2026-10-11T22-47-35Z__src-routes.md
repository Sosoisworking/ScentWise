Specific: amber/cream OKLCH palette with Playfair, note-pyramid pills highlighting matched notes, 173-note taxonomy with live counts, vibes, dark mode, candid About/Editorial voice. ## Overall Impression
The note picker and the "Matches 3 of 4 of your notes" explanation are genuinely good and product-specific. The flow around them leaks: steps open mid-page, results end on "Start Over", and several claims contradict the "honesty is the product" promise. Biggest opportunity: make the results page the honest, Canadian, explainable peak it is meant to be.

## What's Working
- Results explain themselves: "Matches N of M of your notes" with those notes highlighted in the pyramid (src/components/FragranceCard.tsx:107-120, src/components/NotePills.tsx:24-33).
- The note picker: live counts, disabled zero-count chips, vibes that show their notes, inline description of the last-tapped note, real fieldsets, mobile thumb-zone action bar.
- A coherent token-driven visual system with a good dark mode, real Learn photography and a trustworthy editorial voice.

Fix: on step change scroll to the step top and focus its h1 (tabIndex -1), label progress "Step 2 of 6", push a history entry per step, keep answers in sessionStorage. Command: /impeccable harden

Fix: split a decorative tan from an accessible text/fill tone (~oklch(0.50 0.08 60)) or put dark text on tan. Command: /impeccable colorize
- [P1] Honesty contradictions: tier labels by position; About says "four weighted dimensions, season and occasion first" but scoring has five with notes worth 30-50 of 100 (src/utils/scoring.ts:46-53); About promises "sellers worth avoiding" that the guide lacks; Brands "Last updated" is always the current month (src/routes/brands.tsx:75) and its footer cites Fragrantica/aggregators (:246) against About's own-site claim; unsourced "#1 frustration" statistic (src/routes/learn.index.tsx:81) against the Editorial Policy; homepage meta promises a five-step quiz with budget (src/routes/index.tsx:14); "Your perfect scents" vs "a shortlist to sample"; unverified wear-testing claim.
